### 0.2 Active Paradigms

| Paradigm | Key Claim | Strength | Weakness |
|----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Triple-Code Model** (Dehaene) | Numbers processed via visual, verbal, and analog magnitude codes | Strong neural evidence (fMRI, lesion studies) | Does not explain structural notation differences |
| **Embodied Metaphor Theory** (Lakoff and Nunez) | Mathematics grounded in bodily experience via conceptual metaphor | Explains why some notations feel "natural" | Metaphor mappings are post-hoc; hard to falsify |
| **Structural Typology** (Chrisomalis) | Numeral systems classified by structural properties independent of "progress" | Comprehensive historical coverage | Descriptive, not evaluative -- no normative claims |
| **Human Error Theory** (Reason) | Errors arise from latent conditions + active failures in complex systems | Predictive for safety-critical domains | Applied almost exclusively to Western decimal; no cross-system comparison |
| **Cognitive Load Theory** (Sweller) | Working memory limits constrain learning; good design reduces extraneous load | Robust experimental paradigm | Applied to instructional design, not notation design per se |

### 0.3 Methodological Approaches

| Approach | Used By | What It Measures | Gap |
|----------|---------|-----------------|-----|
| Reaction-time experiments | Dehaene, Nieder | Numerical distance effect, SNARC | Compares within-system, not between-system |
| Cross-cultural comparison | Pica et al., Dehaene et al. | Mundurucu vs. Western number line | Ethnographic, not controlled experiment |
| Historical-comparative | Chrisomalis, Cajori | Structural diversity over time | Descriptive, no experimental prediction |
| Error analysis | Cohen, Reason | Medication error rates with decimal notation | Single-system analysis |
| Metaphor analysis | Lakoff and Nunez | Mapping from embodied domains to math | Theoretical, not experimentally tested |

### 0.4 Key Research Questions

1. Can numeral system superiority be measured along multiple independent axes?
2. What trade-offs exist between structural simplicity, cognitive naturalness, and error resistance?
3. Does metaphor-consistent notation design produce measurable learning or performance advantages?
4. Would a dual zero-glyph (cardinal vs. positional zero) reduce conceptual errors?
5. Is base-10 optimal for human cognition, or is it a historical accident?

### 0.5 Domain Topology Verdict

The field is **siloed**: cognitive scientists do not consult historians, human factors researchers do not test non-decimal systems, and semioticians do not run experiments. NUMERATA''s integration claim -- that a multi-axis framework can bridge these silos -- is structurally novel. The question is whether the framework can generate **falsifiable predictions** that any single tradition would miss.

## Stage 1: Paradigm-Shift Candidate Identification

### 1.1 Scoring Rubric

Each candidate scored 1-10 on five dimensions:
- **P** (Probability): Likelihood the shift materializes within 20 years
- **I** (Impact): Magnitude of change if the shift occurs
- **T** (Timeline): Years until measurable impact (lower = sooner)
- **E** (Testability): Ease of designing decisive experiments
- **D** (Dependency): How many prior shifts must occur first (higher = more dependent)

**EV = P * I / (T * D)** -- a rough Expected Value heuristic weighted by testability factor E.

### 1.3 Candidate Detail

#### C1: Multi-Axis Evaluation as Standard
- **Claim:** Within 20 years, numeral system comparison will routinely use multi-axis frameworks (structural, cognitive, design) rather than ad-hoc "which is faster" comparisons.
- **Mechanism:** NUMERATA provides the vocabulary and rubric; follow-up experiments validate specific axes; the framework enters textbooks and design standards.
- **Evidence for:** Growing interdisciplinary interest in notation design; failure of single-metric comparisons in adjacent fields (HCI, typeface design).
- **Evidence against:** Institutional inertia; lack of funding for cross-system comparison; most researchers work within a single paradigm.

#### C2: Metaphor-Consistent Design Advantage
- **Claim:** Numeral systems designed to align with specific embodied metaphors produce 15-30% faster learning and lower error rates in controlled experiments.
- **Mechanism:** Lakoff and Nunez''s metaphor mappings predict which operations feel "natural" in which systems; designing to maximize metaphor alignment reduces cognitive load.
- **Evidence for:** WP0.1''s metaphor recruitment profiles predict differential performance; SNARC effect shows spatial-numerical mapping is real and measurable.
- **Evidence against:** Metaphor theory may be post-hoc rationalization; "naturalness" may be culturally learned, not embodied.

#### C3: Dual Zero-Glyph Intervention
- **Claim:** Teaching children with distinct glyphs for cardinal zero (quantity) and positional zero (placeholder) reduces "zero is nothing" misconceptions.
- **Mechanism:** WP1.1.3''s zero-as-meta-sign analysis predicts that conflating cardinal and positional zero causes conceptual confusion; distinct glyphs make the distinction visible.
- **Evidence for:** Wellman and Miller (1986) show children struggle with zero concepts; Krajcsi and Kojouharova (2020) show zero processing is distinctive.
- **Evidence against:** Adding glyphs increases learning burden; existing notation is deeply entrenched; effect size may be small.

#### C4: Base-12 Superiority for Fractions
- **Claim:** Controlled experiments will show base-12 outperforms base-10 on fraction comparison, addition, and conceptual understanding tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Base-12 terminates 1/3 and 1/4 (vs. base-10 which terminates 1/2 and 1/5 only); fraction termination quality maps directly to cognitive ease.
- **Evidence for:** Mathematical fact of superior fraction termination; historical use of base-12 in trade.
- **Evidence against:** Massive switching cost; base-10 is path-dependent, not optimality-driven; Dehaene''s log-to-linear shift may be base-independent.

#### C5: AI-Optimized Numeral Systems
- **Claim:** Machine learning can discover numeral representations that outperform any historical system for specific human-AI interaction tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Treat numeral design as an optimization problem over the 9-axis taxonomy space; use reinforcement learning with human-in-the-loop feedback.
- **Evidence for:** AI has discovered novel representations in other domains (AlphaFold, AlphaZero); the design space is combinatorially large.
- **Evidence against:** Human cognitive constraints may dominate; "optimal" for AI may differ from "optimal" for humans; 20-year timeline is extremely aggressive.

## Stage 4: Bayesian Sensitivity Analysis

### 4.1 Methodology

For each candidate, we perform three stress tests:
1. **+/-20% Assumption Sensitivity:** Vary each critical assumption''s confidence by +/-20%, observe EV shift. Tornado chart identifies which assumptions dominate.
2. **Halve-Priors:** Cut ALL optimistic prior probabilities by 50% (pessimistic scenario). Does the EV ranking invert?
3. **Correlation Stress-Test:** Assume worst-case correlation between the two most fragile assumptions. If both fail together, does the candidate collapse?

### 4.2 C1: Multi-Axis Evaluation as Standard

**Base EV:** 2.80 (P=7, I=8, T=10, E=8, D=2)

#### Assumption Sensitivity Tornado

| Assumption | Base Conf | +20% EV | -20% EV | EV Swing | Rank |
|-----------|-----------|---------|---------|----------|------|
| A1.2 (Multi-axis produces different rankings) | 0.85 | 3.05 | 2.55 | +/-0.25 | 1 |
| A1.4 (Shared vocabulary adopted) | 0.45 | 3.15 | 2.50 | +/-0.33 | 1 |
| A1.1 (Axes are independent) | 0.75 | 2.95 | 2.65 | +/-0.15 | 3 |
| A1.3 (Falsifiable predictions) | 0.70 | 2.90 | 2.70 | +/-0.10 | 4 |
| A1.5 (Funding exists — NON-CRITICAL) | 0.30 | — | — | — | — |

**Key insight:** The EV is most sensitive to A1.4 (shared vocabulary — confidence only 0.45) and A1.2 (different rankings — high confidence but high leverage). If researchers cannot agree on shared vocabulary, the entire framework collapses even if all other assumptions hold.

#### Halve-Priors Scenario

Halving all probabilities (P: 7→3.5, all confidences halved):
- P_effective: 3.5 × (0.5×0.85) × (0.5×0.45) × (0.5×0.75) × (0.5×0.70) = 3.5 × 0.425 × 0.225 × 0.375 × 0.350 = 3.5 × 0.0125 ≈ 0.044
- Adjusted EV ≈ 0.044 × 8 / (10 × 2) × 8 ≈ **0.14**

**Verdict:** Under severe pessimism, C1 becomes a LOW EV candidate. The framework''s success depends critically on several moderate-confidence assumptions all holding.

#### Correlation Stress-Test

Assume A1.4 (shared vocabulary, conf 0.45) and A1.1 (axis independence, conf 0.75) fail together — i.e., the axes are correlated AND researchers won''t adopt shared vocabulary:

- If axes are correlated, multi-axis rankings collapse toward single-axis rankings
- If no shared vocabulary, cross-paradigm work is impossible
- **Combined failure probability:** P(both fail) = (1-0.45) × (1-0.75) × correlation_factor(0.3) ≈ 0.55 × 0.25 × 0.3 ≈ 0.041
- **Impact if both fail:** EV → ~0.30 (framework becomes descriptive taxonomy, no adoption)

**Verdict:** C1 is robust to single-assumption failure but fragile to combined failure of the two most uncertain assumptions.
